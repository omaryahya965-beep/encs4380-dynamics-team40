Understanding Module 2

Why is this a first-order system?

The model τ dy/dt + y = Kx contains one time derivative. Here K = 1, so a constant input is eventually reproduced without a gain change.

What does the time constant mean?

A larger τ means a slower sensor. At the same nonzero frequency, it gives a smaller amplitude ratio and a larger phase lag. Its cutoff frequency is lower.

How do Hz and rad/s differ?

Frequency f counts cycles per second. Angular frequency ω counts radians per second: ω = 2πf. An input sin(2t) has ω = 2 rad/s, or about 0.318 Hz.

What does phase lag mean?

The output reaches its peaks later than the input. A negative phase shift indicates lag. For a single sine wave, the time delay is −φ/ω when φ is in radians.

What happens at cutoff?

At ω = 1/τ, M = 1/√2 ≈ 0.707 and φ = −45°. The Bode magnitude is about −3.01 dB. The sensor still responds above cutoff, but with more attenuation.

What does dynamic error measure?

δ = M − 1 measures the relative amplitude error for this unity-gain model. Multiply by 100 for percent. It does not include phase error. The sine plots show steady state, after the startup transient has faded.
